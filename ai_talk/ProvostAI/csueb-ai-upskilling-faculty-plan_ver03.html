Faculty To‑Do Lists to Help Students Upskill with AI (CSU East Bay)

Author

Department of Statistics & Data Science — CSU East Bay

Published

September 20, 2025

1 Purpose

A concise, faculty‑facing plan—derived from the Provost’s Back to the Bay 2025 keynote—covering key ideas and concrete actions we can embed in Statistics and Data Science courses to upskill students.

Reference keynote: Back to the Bay Keynote Script – AI + Workforce Development, August 13–14, 2025. Office of Academic Affairs, CSU East Bay. (link in References).

2 Executive Summary of the Provost’s Vision

2.1 What the Provost Means by “Upskilling”

  • Deliberately adding new capabilities and mindsets that prepare students to apply knowledge in unfamiliar situations—not just listing more skills on a résumé. Provost Keynote
  • Goal: Graduates who learn quickly, communicate effectively, exercise judgment, and work across disciplines in a world where the only constant is change. Provost Keynote
  • Motivation: AI is redefining “entry‑level” work; employers expect more complex, judgment‑based tasks earlier in careers. Provost Keynote

2.2 Three-Part Framework

  1. AI Fluency — Understand how AI works, what it can and cannot do, and how to apply it responsibly. Provost Keynote
  2. Human‑Centric Skills — Empathy, ethics, communication, teamwork, leadership, and professionalism that AI cannot replace. Provost Keynote
  3. Agility — The capacity to adapt quickly when conditions change and to course‑correct with confidence. Provost Keynote

3 Main Areas the Keynote Highlighted for Higher Education

  • Demographic & trust headwinds for higher education; we must prove value and improve student readiness earlier in the curriculum. Provost Keynote
  • AI adoption in industry is accelerating; “entry‑level” expectations are moving toward judgment, communication, and collaboration. Provost Keynote
  • We must intentionally cultivate: AI fluency, human‑centric skills, and agility across courses, co‑curriculars, community projects, and research. Provost Keynote

4 Main Areas for the Future Job Market (CSUEB Students)

  • Employers seek more than technical competence: critical thinking, clear communication to diverse audiences, ethical judgment, collaboration with people and digital tools, professionalism, curiosity, and relational intelligence. Provost Keynote
  • Experience gap matters as much as skills: students need realistic, messy contexts (people, deadlines, shifting priorities) to practice judgment. Provost Keynote
