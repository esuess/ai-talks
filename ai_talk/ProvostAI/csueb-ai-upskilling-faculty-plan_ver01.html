Faculty To‑Do Lists to Help Students Upskill with AI (CSU East Bay)

Author

Department of Statistics & Data Science — CSU East Bay

Published

September 20, 2025

1 Purpose

A concise, faculty‑facing plan—derived from the Provost’s Back to the Bay 2025 keynote—covering key ideas and concrete actions we can embed in Statistics and Data Science courses to upskill students.

Reference keynote: Back to the Bay Keynote Script – AI + Workforce Development, August 13–14, 2025. Office of Academic Affairs, CSU East Bay. (link in References).

2 Executive Summary of the Provost’s Vision

2.1 What the Provost Means by “Upskilling”

  • Deliberately adding new capabilities and mindsets that prepare students to apply knowledge in unfamiliar situations—not just listing more skills on a résumé.
  • Goal: Graduates who learn quickly, communicate effectively, exercise judgment, and work across disciplines in a world where the only constant is change.
  • Motivation: AI is redefining “entry‑level” work; employers expect more complex, judgment‑based tasks earlier in careers.

2.2 Three-Part Framework

  1. AI Fluency — Understand how AI works, what it can and cannot do, and how to apply it responsibly.
  2. Human‑Centric Skills — Empathy, ethics, communication, teamwork, leadership, and professionalism that AI cannot replace.
  3. Agility — The capacity to adapt quickly when conditions change and to course‑correct with confidence.

3 Main Areas the Keynote Highlighted for Higher Education

  • Demographic & trust headwinds for higher education; we must prove value and improve student readiness earlier in the curriculum.
  • AI adoption in industry is accelerating; “entry‑level” expectations are moving toward judgment, communication, and collaboration.
  • We must intentionally cultivate: AI fluency, human‑centric skills, and agility across courses, co‑curriculars, community projects, and research.

4 Main Areas for the Future Job Market (CSUEB Students)

  • Employers seek more than technical competence: critical thinking, clear communication to diverse audiences, ethical judgment, collaboration with people and digital tools, professionalism, curiosity, and relational intelligence.
  • Experience gap matters as much as skills: students need realistic, messy contexts (people, deadlines, shifting priorities) to practice judgment.

5 Faculty To‑Do Lists by Area

5.1 1. AI Fluency — What It Means

For students: - Baseline literacy in model capabilities/limits, data provenance, prompting & verification, privacy & security, and responsible use. - Ability to choose and justify when to use (or not use) AI tools.

For faculty: - Integrate scaffolded, low‑stakes uses of AI with explicit guardrails, attribution, and evaluation standards.

5.1.1 Faculty To‑Do (Statistics & Data Science)

  • Syllabus & Policy
    • Add an AI use policy: permitted tools (e.g., ChatGPT Edu, code copilots), disclosure/attribution, verification steps, and privacy expectations.
    • Create a simple decision rubric (“use AI / do not use AI / use with verification”) for common task types.
  • In‑Class Micro‑Labs (15–25 min)
    • Prompt‑engineering warmups: design and test prompts for EDA, data cleaning, simulation setup, then compare outputs.
    • Model limits demo: adversarial cases (e.g., extrapolation beyond data, spurious correlations) and error analysis.
  • Assignments
    • Require AI‑aided and AI‑free versions of selected tasks; students submit a tool log and a verification note.
    • Add reproducibility checkpoints: students must re‑run code end‑to‑end; attach a session info block.
  • Assessment
    • Include viva‑style checks or short code‑explanations to validate understanding beyond AI outputs.
    • Use rubrics that reward method choice, assumption checks, and interpretation, not just final answers.

5.2 2. Human‑Centric Skills — What It Means

For students: - Practice empathy, ethics, teamwork, leadership, and clear communication under constraints and stakeholder diversity.

For faculty: - Design tasks where the human lens is essential: ambiguity, trade‑offs, equity, and audience‑aware storytelling.

5.2.1 Faculty To‑Do (Statistics & Data Science)

  • Communication Across Audiences
    • Require a two‑pass deliverable: (a) technical memo; (b) one‑page brief for a non‑technical stakeholder.
    • Add visual narrative checks: students must justify figure choices (markings, scales, uncertainty displays).
  • Ethics & Equity
    • Short ethics prompts tied to content (e.g., bias in training data, disparate impact, data privacy). Students propose mitigations.
    • Include a model risk statement with each project: assumptions, failure modes, potential harm, and monitoring.
  • Teamwork & Professionalism
    • Structured team roles: Lead, Reviewer, Communicator, Scribe—rotate mid‑project.
    • Use issue trackers/Kanban for deadlines; grade process artifacts (meeting notes, task boards).
  • Mentoring Moments
    • Implement peer code‑reviews and peer feedback rounds with a short reflective memo on received feedback.

5.3 3. Agility — What It Means

For students: - Comfort with changing requirements, new data, and iteration; ability to pivot while preserving rigor.

For faculty: - Create time‑boxed sprints, mid‑stream requirement changes, and A/B comparisons of solution paths.

5.3.1 Faculty To‑Do (Statistics & Data Science)

  • Agility Sprints
    • Run 1–2 week sprints where the data generating process shifts mid‑sprint.
    • Include a retro: what changed, how the team adapted, what to automate next time.
  • Model‑Change Drills
    • Provide a baseline model; then supply new features or shifted distributions and assess update speed and validation.
  • Tooling
    • Encourage notebooks + scripts + Makefiles (or targets in R) for re‑runnable pipelines under change.

6 Experiential Learning Activities (by Area)

Design for real‑world context, stakeholders, deadlines, and evolving constraints. Where AI assists, require verification and reflection.

6.1 AI Fluency — Activity Ideas

  • AI‑Aided EDA & Verification Lab
    Students use AI to propose EDA steps on a noisy dataset; they must verify at least three AI claims with code and explain any discrepancies.

  • Baseline vs. AI‑Assisted Forecasting
    Compare a classical model and an AI‑assisted approach on a time series. Evaluate with \(\text{{RMSE}} = \sqrt{{\frac1n\sum_i (\hat{{y}}_i - y_i)^2}}\) and report pros/cons.

  • Prompt‑to‑Pipeline
    Start from an English prompt; produce a minimal, reproducible R pipeline (e.g., targets) with unit tests for key functions.

6.2 Human‑Centric Skills — Activity Ideas

  • Stakeholder Brief + Technical Appendix
    Teams analyze a dataset with equity implications (e.g., admissions, lending). Deliver: (1) a one‑page policy brief; (2) a technical appendix with sensitivity analysis and uncertainty intervals \(\left[\hat{{\theta}} \pm z_{{0.975}}\,\widehat{{\mathrm{{SE}}}}(\hat{{\theta}})\right]\).

  • Ethics Case Clinic
    Short cases where AI output conflicts with community values. Students propose a decision rule, e.g., cost‑sensitive loss
    \[L = c_1\,\mathbf{{1}}\{{\text{{FP}}\}} + c_2\,\mathbf{{1}}\{{\text{{FN}}\}}\]
    and justify \(c_1, c_2\) for a chosen stakeholder.

  • Peer Review & Relational Intelligence
    Structured code/document reviews with actionable feedback; students track how revisions improved clarity and fairness.

6.3 Agility — Activity Ideas

  • Concept Drift Response Drill
    Provide train/test from \(P_0\); then reveal test‑2 from \(P_1\) with drift. Students must: detect drift, re‑tune, and justify a deployment gate (e.g., stop if AUC drops \(>5\%\)).

  • Live Data Hackday
    Pull an open API; mid‑day, change the KPI. Students pivot, update pipeline, and present a retro on trade‑offs.

  • Rapid Experimentation Plan
    Students design an A/B test with power calculation
    \[n = 2\,\left(\frac{{z_{{1-\alpha/2}} + z_{{1-\beta}}}}{{\delta/\sigma}}\right)^2,\]
    and discuss operational risks (ethics, data quality, monitoring).

7 Alignment with CSUEB Programs

  • Statistics B.S. — Embed AI‑assisted EDA, ethics mini‑prompts, and agility sprints in regression, probability, and capstone.
  • Statistics M.S. — Add concept‑drift labs, model‑risk statements, and stakeholder briefs in applied projects.
  • Data Science B.S. — Use prompt‑to‑pipeline, reproducible MLOps practices, and live API hackdays across the curriculum.

8 Implementation Timeline (One Semester)

  • Weeks 1–2: Publish AI policy & rubrics; run AI‑fluency micro‑labs.
  • Weeks 3–7: Launch first team project with stakeholder brief + ethics prompts; add peer reviews.
  • Weeks 8–10: Agility sprint with mid‑course requirement change; model‑change drill.
  • Weeks 11–14: Capstone‑style project; include experience‑gap elements (deadlines, role rotation, external feedback).
  • Finals: Oral validations; reflective memo on AI use, verification, and professional growth.
